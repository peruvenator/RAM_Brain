# Return Stacked Writing Style

Apply the Return Stacked voice to any public-facing RS content.

## Canonical source

Before drafting or revising, read the full style guide bundled with this skill:

`references/RS_Writing_STYLE_GUIDE.md`

That file is the authority. It covers voice/identity, argumentation architecture, sentence-level craft, vocabulary, document-type templates, citation style, and required disclaimers. This skill surfaces the non-negotiables and the artifact template to use; the full guide is the rulebook.

## When to use

- RS blog posts (standard 1,500-3,000 words; research-style 3,000-6,000+)
- Academic reviews (800-1,100 words)
- Product briefs (800-1,000 words plus legal)
- Advisor guides (1,600-3,200 words)
- Market commentary / "Why Now" pieces (1,600-1,900 words)
- Whitepapers (7,000-8,000+ words)
- Landing page copy, advisor-facing emails, social posts, ad copy
- Ghostwriting for Corey, Adam, or Rodrigo when speaking in RS capacity

## Not for

- Internal Slack or team comms
- ReSolve AM-branded content (different voice)
- Rodrigo's personal writing
- Internal memos, decisions, or project notes

## Non-negotiables (check every output)

- **No em dashes.** Never. Replace with commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, or separate sentences. Search the final draft for `—` and `--` before delivery.
- **"We / our" by default.** First person singular "I" only appears in hypothetical investor voices ("Have I missed the bus on return stacking?"). Second person "you / your" when addressing the advisor's practice.
- **Earned confidence.** Bold on concepts, mechanics, math, and historical facts. Hedged on anything forward-looking ("may," "can," "seeks to," "potentially," "historically," "tends to").
- **Lead with honesty, not sales.** "Here's the answer, and here's why you probably shouldn't use it" is the preferred posture. No hype.
- **Every paragraph earns its place.** Cut anything that exists only to sound thorough.
- **Oxford comma, always.**
- **Spell out numbers under ten; numerals for 10+.** Always numerals with percentages, units, and financial figures.

## Core structural moves

- **"Not X, but Y" reframe.** Validate the conventional view first, then reframe. Never combative.
- **"And not or" / "Yes and."** Dissolve false dichotomies. "We advocate for saying 'and' not 'or'."
- **Steelman-concede-recontextualize.** Name the objection, concede real validity, then reframe it (often as a named cognitive bias: line-item bias, leverage myopia, product myopia).
- **Signature sentence pattern: "By [gerund], [subject] can [verb]."** Use 2-4 times per article.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Plain-language first, numerical example second, math last. Concrete before abstract.

## Vocabulary cheatsheet

**Prefer:** return stacking, capital efficient, diversifying return streams, core portfolio, risk-adjusted returns, structural alpha, crisis alpha, outperform benchmarks, yield / carry / income component, line-item risk, the funding problem, 100/100 notation, $1.00 of exposure for each $1.00 invested.

**Avoid:** leveraged allocation, leveraged (when framing positively), diversified investments (too vague), Sharpe ratio returns, alternative risk premia (as a category label), security-selection alpha, hedge (too vague), beat the market, passive income, main/base portfolio, merger arb (in formal content).

**Never use:** gambling, speculation (for RS strategies), guaranteed, exotic, complex (for return stacking itself), get rich, simple (for the overall concept), sports metaphors, war metaphors.

**Brand mark:** "return stacking" lowercase for the concept. "Return Stacked(R)" or "Return Stacked(R)" title case with (R) on first mention for the brand/product line.

## Artifact flow templates

Pick the template matching the artifact type. Each has a rigid flow documented in the full guide.

| Artifact | Flow signature |
|---|---|
| Blog post | Problem > Concept > Evidence/Data > Practical Implication > Conclusion > Key Takeaways (bulleted, bold lead-ins) |
| Academic review | Bibliographic header > Paper summary > Key insights with figures > Implications for Return Stacking Strategies > Conclusion |
| Product brief | Hook question > What is Return Stacking > Allocation chart > Why [Strategy] > Drawdowns > Correlations > Growth of $100 > Practical implementation > Social proof > Notes/Definitions/Glossary/Risk Factors |
| Advisor guide | Why This Conversation Matters > What Is [Strategy] > Key Analogy > How to Talk to Clients About [Strategy] (Q&A) > Quick-Reference Bullet Points > Managing [Strategy] with Return Stacking > Conclusion |
| Market commentary | Provocative timely observation > Macro thesis > Evidence > Return stacking reframe > Humility caveat > Philosophical close |
| Whitepaper | TOC > Definitions > Experimental Design > Data > Methodology > Results > Scenario Analysis > Stacking Case Studies > Conclusion > References > Appendix |

## Required disclaimers on every figure

- "Returns are gross of all fees, taxes, and transaction costs."
- "Performance assumes the reinvestment of all distributions."
- "Past performance is not indicative of future results."
- "You cannot invest in an index." (or "Investors cannot invest directly in an index.")
- "For illustrative purposes only."

**Source line by authorship:**
- Newfound-authored: "Source: [Data Provider]. Calculations by Newfound Research."
- ReSolve-authored: "Source: ReSolve Asset Management Inc., [Data Providers]. Analysis by ReSolve Asset Management SEZC (Cayman)."

## Workflow

1. Read the canonical guide linked above
2. Identify the artifact type and pull its flow template
3. Draft or revise
4. Self-review against the non-negotiables list
5. Final scan: search the draft for `—` and `--` to kill any em dashes
6. Verify every forward-looking claim has a hedge and every figure has the required disclaimers
